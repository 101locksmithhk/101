Real case・Tai Wai, Sha Tin District・Residential wooden door

Tai Wai lock replacement｜An ageing three-turn wooden-door lock had become stiff

101 Locksmith・29 October 2026

On 29 October, a Tai Wai resident contacted us about a wooden-door lock that had been in use for years. Opening and locking had gradually become less smooth. The key still worked, but the customer was concerned that the lock might eventually jam, so an inspection was arranged before it failed.

Why deal with a lock before it fails completely?

This wooden door is used every day. Although the existing lock had not stopped working, its operation already felt different. Continuing to force the key could allow the problem to worsen until the door could no longer be opened normally.

The customer had two questions: whether only the cylinder needed attention and whether fitting a new lock would affect the existing wooden door. Stiff key operation alone does not identify the cause, so the actual condition of the lock had to be checked first.

The cylinder was secured before fitting the new 99 rim lock

The locksmith first discussed how the lock behaved during daily use, then inspected the existing three-turn lock. Once the ageing condition was confirmed, the job was completed in two parts: securing the cylinder and fitting a new 99 rim lock.

The work focused on restoring smooth operation while retaining as much of the existing wooden-door structure as possible. The new lock was then checked for normal opening and locking.

The customer tested the lock after installation

After installation, the locksmith tested opening, closing and locking, then asked the customer to try the new lock. The earlier stiffness had been resolved and the new 99 rim lock operated smoothly.

Replace the cylinder or the complete wooden-door lock?

If the lock body works normally and the aim is simply to change the keys after moving home, a tenancy change or possible key loss, replacing only the cylinder may be suitable. If the internal parts are worn, the latch is sticking or the complete lock has aged, a cylinder change alone may not solve the problem.

In this Tai Wai case: the fault involved an ageing lock, so the cylinder was secured and a new 99 rim lock was fitted.

How is a Tai Wai wooden-door lock replacement priced?

Pricing depends on the lock type, replacement scope, condition of the existing cylinder and lock body, required parts and call-out time. Photos of the lock face, door edge and latch, together with a description of the problem, help with the initial assessment.

Do not keep forcing a three-turn lock that has become harder to operate. Humidity, loose hinges or a sagging door can affect the alignment between the latch and strike, so the cylinder may not be the only issue.
